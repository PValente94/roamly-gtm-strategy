What I built

Five modules, one go-to-market plan

Each module added one decision. Together they set who Roamly Groups is for, how it wins and how it makes money.

Module 1
GTM strategy
Target segment

Groups of friends of young adults aged 21 to 35 travelling together, would be the most valuable segment to target first.

WHY: This age band is highly active on social media and travel communities, making them more likely to share experiences and generate word-of-mouth growth for booking for large groups using Roamly. They also tend to be more flexible when it comes to organizing and joining group trips due to generally having less responsibilities, are comfortable with digital booking experiences, and are ideal candidates to value split payments - since younger travelers often have more limited disposable income, the ability to share costs across a group could be a particularly compelling value proposition.

GTM motion

The recommended GTM strategy is a hybrid approach, with Product-Led Growth (PLG) as the primary motion and Community-Led Growth (CLG) as the supporting motion.

PLG leads because demand for large-group bookings already exists, as evidenced by high drop-off rates. The opportunity is to remove booking barriers, improve conversion, and capture more value from existing customers.

CLG complements PLG because Roamly's brand is rooted in trust, authenticity, and community-driven discovery. Group bookings create shared experiences that naturally generate referrals, user-generated content, and word-of-mouth growth.

Together, PLG drives expansion by converting unmet demand, while CLG drives acquisition through community advocacy and trusted recommendations.

Module 2
Battlecard and strategic bet
Primary competitor

Airbnb Experiences

Strategic bet

I am betting that Roamly Groups wins by becoming the easiest way for groups of friends to discover, coordinate, and pay for authentic local experiences. By eliminating the need for a single organizer to manage bookings and front the entire cost, we can unlock a major source of unmet demand while staying true to Roamly's community-driven roots and focus on locally curated experiences.

Deprioritizing

My trade-off is clear: I will prioritize friend groups seeking authentic, locally curated experiences, even if that means deprioritizing larger opportunities such as corporate offsites, team-building events, bachelor and bachelorette parties, or other highly commercialized group travel categories. Even if future data comes to suggest these segments offer a larger addressable market, pursuing them would pull Roamly away from its core differentiator: helping travelers discover unique local experiences through trusted recommendations and community-driven discovery. I am choosing differentiation and brand alignment over market breadth.

Module 3
Positioning
Target audience

Friend groups aged between 21 and 35 planning a holiday together who want to easily book trusted local experiences without the complexity of managing separate accounts, coordinating payments, or piecing together itineraries.

Positioning statement

Tired of being the person who has to pay for everyone and chase friends for money? Roamly Groups lets groups book authentic local experiences together, with split payments, shared itineraries, and group coordination built directly into the booking experience.

Module 4
Messaging pillars
From/To shift

From: Planning a group experience means one person becomes the organizer, collecting money, coordinating decisions, chasing confirmations, and keeping everyone informed across multiple apps and conversations.

To: Booking a group experience becomes a shared process, where everyone can see the plans, contribute their payment, and stay aligned from booking through the experience.

Audience 1 pillar

For: The person who usually organizes and books experiences on behalf of the group.

Value prop:
- Spend less time booking for your travel group and more time enjoying the experience with them.

Capabilities:
- Split payments across participants
- Shared itineraries
- Centralized booking management
- Group visibility into booking status

Evidence:

- 92% of organizers say booking with the group is more fun
- reduces organizers time spent coordinating bookings and payments in 58%
- 107 verified group organizers came back within 6 months to book a new group experience

Copy across three surfaces

Module 5
Pricing recommendation
Goal and model

Goal: Grow revenue from a known audience
Model: Transaction-based
Who pays: Roamly Group customers pay a fee on top of the local experience price they are booking. Fee is per transaction, meaning per activity being booked.

Tiers

- Good

Name - Solo; Who's it for - single travellers booking their own local experience or the one group organizer that books it all for its group of friends; Rough price: 3% fee on top of local experience advertised price

- Better

Name - Group's Favorite; Who's it for - groups that want to book an experience leveraging split payments and booking details like shared itineraries; Rough price: 5% fee on top of local experience advertised price

- Best
Name - Premium; Who's it for - groups that want a semi-automated booking experience, with personalized travel recommendations based on group preferences and planned travel routes, all without losing perks of "Group's favourite"; Rough price: 8% fee on top of local experience advertised price

Assumption to test

Large groups are willing to pay more for the convenience of split payments, shared itineraries, and group-wide booking visibility because these features reduce coordination effort while keeping every traveler informed and engaged throughout the experience.

Reflection

What I learned

Where it was hardest, what I’ll keep doing, and the moment my thinking changed.

Friction

Keep up with the amount of concepts (and their variations) that are relevant when launching a new product to market. Given some of these were new for me there is a lot to digest. But I can always come back to the materials after the course.

Key learnings

- thinking and documenting the whole strategy through before working on the solution
- use provided tools and examples to optimize for pricing
- coming back to concepts and GTM framework
- using GBB and the philosophy behind it: shifting the customer’s decision from “do I buy this?” to “which version is right for me?”, which is a whole new framing for me as a PM as well

Aha moment

My biggest aha moment was understanding how much responsibility a PM has beyond the product itself. Successful launches require thorough thinking and proactive planning to ensure every team has the clarity, information, and support needed to do their job well. So it is also my responsibility to make them succeed, only then can I be successful as well.
