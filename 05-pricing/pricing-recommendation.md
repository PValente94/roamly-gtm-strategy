# Pricing Recommendation · Roamly Groups

## 1. Pricing goal

- **Goal:** Grow revenue from a known audience

## 2. Pricing model

- **Model:** Transaction-based
- **Who pays, and for what:** Roamly Group customers pay a fee on top of the local experience price they are booking. Fee is per transaction, meaning per activity being booked.

## 3. Packaging

- **Tiers:**
  - Good:
  - Name - Solo; Who's it for - single travellers booking their own local experience or the one group organizer that books it all for its group of friends; Rough price: 3% fee on top of local experience advertised price
  - Better:
  - Name - Groups' Favorite; Who's it for - groups that want to book an experience leveraging split payments and booking details like shared itineraries; Rough price: 5% fee on top of local experience advertised price
  - Best:
  - Name - Premium; Who's it for - groups that want a semi-automated booking experience, with personalized travel recommendations based on group preferences and planned travel routes, all without losing perks of "Group's favourite"; Rough price: 8% fee on top of local experience advertised price

## 4. Assumption to test

- **The one assumption I would test before launch:** Large groups are willing to pay more for the convenience of split payments, shared itineraries, and group-wide booking visibility because these features reduce coordination effort while keeping every traveler informed and engaged throughout the experience.

## 5. One-page recommendation

### Roamly Groups Pricing Recommendation (V1)

#### Objective

**Grow revenue from an existing audience** by monetizing large-group bookings that currently experience high drop-off due to payment and coordination friction.

---

#### Recommended Pricing Model

**Transaction-based pricing**

Customers pay a fee on top of the local experience price for each experience booked.

Why:

- Aligns pricing with value delivered
- Customers only pay when a booking happens
- Revenue scales with booking volume
- Simple to understand and explain

---

#### Pricing Tiers

| Tier | Audience | Core Value | Fee |
|--------|--------|--------|--------|
| **Solo** | Individual travelers or one organizer paying for everyone | Standard booking | **3%** |
| **Group's Favorite** | Groups using split payments and shared itineraries | Easier coordination and payment management | **5%** |
| **Premium** | Groups wanting recommendations and itinerary assistance | Personalized group travel planning | **8%** |

---

#### Stakeholder View

##### Finance

**What matters most**

- Revenue grows with booking volume
- Higher monetization on higher-value group bookings
- No subscription adoption risk
- Captures value from bookings that might otherwise be abandoned

##### Sales Leadership

**What matters most**

Simple story:

> "Everyone pays their share. Nobody has to front the entire booking."

This makes differentiation against Airbnb Experiences easy to explain and justify.

##### Product Marketing

**What matters most**

We are not charging for features.

We are charging for:

- Less payment chasing
- Less coordination effort
- More transparency
- Better group travel experiences

**Recommended message**

> *Book authentic local experiences together without becoming the group's bank.*

---

#### Key Assumption

> We assume large groups are willing to pay a premium for split payments, shared itineraries, and booking visibility because these capabilities reduce coordination effort and remove the financial burden from the organizer.

---

#### Key Risks

##### Risk 1

Customers view split payments as a standard feature rather than a premium benefit.

**Mitigation:** Bundle split payments with broader coordination features and emphasize the time saved.

##### Risk 2

Customers compare the fee against booking directly with providers.

**Mitigation:** Focus messaging on convenience, transparency, and reduced organizer burden.

##### Risk 3

Premium tier demand is uncertain.

**Mitigation:** Test willingness-to-pay before significant investment in recommendation capabilities.

---

#### Recommended Validation Method

##### ✅ Conjoint Analysis

**Why Conjoint?**

The core question is not:

> "What price should we charge?"

The core question is:

> "How much are customers willing to pay for split payments, shared itineraries, booking visibility, and recommendations?"

Conjoint analysis will help determine:

- Willingness to pay for each feature
- Price sensitivity
- Most attractive tier structure
- Whether Premium features justify a higher fee

##### Why not Van Westendorp?

Good for determining a price range for a single offer, but not for evaluating feature trade-offs.

##### Why not Max-Diff?

Good for ranking features, but not for measuring willingness to pay.

---

#### Recommendation

Launch with a **transaction-based model**, position **Group's Favorite (5%)** as the hero tier, and use **Conjoint Analysis** to validate willingness to pay before expanding the Premium offering.
